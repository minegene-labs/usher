# Features in detail

How each part of Usher behaves. The words used here are defined in [glossary.md](glossary.md); every setting named here is in [configuration.md](configuration.md).

## The journey of a request

| Step | Usher shows | Comes from |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Asked | Searching…, then Looking for it / Still looking (after 3 days, shown calmly: it's not a problem), or Not out yet for a movie or show (or season) that hasn't been released or aired. Usher asks Sonarr/Radarr to search again after 1 hour, every 6 hours for 3 days, then daily, since their feeds never carry older titles. A new episode of a show you already have that aired a few hours ago and still has no file is searched for the same way, even after the request is finished | Usher's own request record |
| Found | Found it, waiting to start | Sonarr/Radarr queue |
| Downloading | Downloading 45% · about 12 min left; for a show partly here, "16 of 28 ready, 12 downloading"; 45% · waiting for someone to share it after 3 hours without progress (calm, not a problem; Usher keeps trying and Stop downloading stays available); "may be the wrong show" when the release name doesn't mention the title (any of its alternate titles counts) | Transmission, every 2 s |
| Getting it ready | Getting it ready, then Adding to Jellyfin | Queue import state, then the library file |
| Ready to watch | Ready to watch | Jellyfin has the item (matched by TVDB, TMDb or IMDb id, or by folder) |

The **Downloads** tab (a sidebar item on a wide screen, with a badge counting what is new since the tab was last opened on this device: a new request, a download that finished or one with a problem; opening the tab clears it, and the tab itself always shows the live list) holds everything from asked to ready to watch: requests made in Usher, then downloads nobody asked for here (new episodes of followed shows). **Stop downloading** (on a download, or a request's journey while it's downloading) cancels it in Transmission, deletes the partial files, and switches off monitoring for just those episodes or that movie so it isn't fetched again; anything already here stays, and a request with nothing here is dropped. **Family-friendly folders** (optional): set `tv_folder` and `movie_folder` under `[family]` in `usher.toml` to separate library folders (also added as root folders in Sonarr and Radarr) and Get it files every title with a family-friendly rating (`ratings`; G, PG, TV-Y, TV-Y7, TV-Y7-FV, TV-G or TV-PG by default) there by itself, with nothing to choose; a missing family-friendly folder is an error, never a silent fallback to the main one, and the wiring check covers both. **Stop looking for it** (on a request's journey while it's still searching) withdraws it without deleting anything: a movie or show with no files leaves Radarr/Sonarr, and a season asked for on a show already here is just switched off with its missing episodes. Anything new in the library (not opened yet) also shows in a **New** row at the very top of Home, newest first, with its count and **Mark all as read**, which clears the row, every new-item dot and the app icon badge. Opening an item takes it out of the row; with nothing new the row isn't there.

**Stalled downloads are replaced by themselves** (`[downloads]` in `usher.toml`). When a download Sonarr or Radarr started has made no progress for 6 hours (nobody sharing it, or it never started), Usher asks Sonarr or Radarr for the releases they would accept for that episode, season or movie, so the quality profile, blocklist and their other rules still apply. If one names the right title, covers the same thing (a season pack for a season pack), is at most twice the size and has clearly more people sharing it (at least 5, and 3 times as many), it's started alongside the stalled one, and both show **Trying a better copy**. Whichever is added to the library first stays and seeds as usual; the other is removed from Transmission with its files (the stalled one is also blocklisted), and Usher checks nothing is left behind. Limits: a replacement starts only if, after it and everything still downloading, at least 20 GB more than Sonarr's and Radarr's own minimum stays free; one per title at a time, 2 at once, 3 per title (then it waits 3 days); never for a download paused by someone, added by hand, or over 90% done and still moving. No notifications. Every decision is in Status → Logs. It starts in **report** mode (`replace = "report"`), which only logs what it would do; set `replace = "on"` once those lines look right, or `"off"`. Set `folders` to Transmission's download folders as Usher sees them to have them checked for leftovers.

TV choices map to Sonarr's monitor options: *Latest season* → `latestSeason` + search, *From the start* → `all` + search, *New episodes only* → `future`, no search. Movies are added at the HD-1080p profile and searched straight away. **Try again** removes and blocklists the stuck copy and searches for another; **Remove** deletes through Sonarr/Radarr into their recycle bin (the folder `setup.sh` asks for), emptied after 7 days.

**Storage** (Status) shows how much of the library disk is used, split into Movies, TV, Family libraries (when the family folders are set), Downloads, Recycle bin and Other, with the free space below. The split comes from the sizes Sonarr and Radarr report, what the downloader has fetched and the recycle bin; files the downloader shares with the library are counted once.

**Tidy up** (Status → Storage) lists movies and whole seasons the viewing account (`media_server.user_id`) has watched to the end, biggest first; a season still airing, one with an episode unwatched, or anything downloading is left out. Nothing goes by itself: **Remove…** on one switches off its monitoring (so it isn't fetched again) and deletes its files through Sonarr/Radarr into the 7-day recycle bin. Files still seeding free their space only once seeding ends. **Keep** takes a movie or a whole show (every season, later ones too) off the list and out of the space figure; **Kept titles**, under the list, shows them with **Put back**, which lists a title again if it still qualifies. Keeping is shared by everyone on the server and stored by TMDb or TVDB ID in `kept.json` in the data folder; it deletes nothing, and a kept title that leaves the server just stops being listed.

**Everything stays in step.** Each title has one identity on the server (`usher/titles.py`), keyed by its TMDb id (movies) or TVDB id (shows) and linked to its Sonarr/Radarr id, its folder, its Jellyfin items and its requests. Every change to a title goes through it: Get it, Remove, Tidy up, a move into the family-friendly folders, and changes the poller notices in Sonarr, Radarr and Jellyfin (a title added or deleted in their own pages, new files, a watched episode). The server then drops its own copies of that title (Search, Library, Upcoming, Tidy up) and sends every open device a `changed` live update, which drops the device's copies too (Library, Upcoming, Discover, a show's seasons, an open details sheet, Search) before the new Home arrives. A removed title leaves Home at once, Continue watching, Next up and Latest included: Jellyfin is told its files have gone, and until it has caught up Usher leaves it out. A request whose movie or show was deleted in Sonarr or Radarr is closed rather than searched for again, and a request's Jellyfin item is checked on every look, so a title that moves keeps working **Open in the app**. Poster addresses carry the picture's version, so a new poster shows straight away while unchanged ones stay on the device.

On the server, `/healthz` also lists `attention`: only things a person has to act on, such as a download that may be the wrong title, one line each, for the daily check. A slow or stalled download isn't listed; a better copy that couldn't be tried for lack of space, or a replaced copy that left files behind (with their path), is.

## Discover

With a free TMDb token in `.env` (`TMDB_TOKEN`: the **API Read Access Token** from TMDb's API settings, sent as a `Bearer` header so it stays out of web addresses and logs; the older 32-character API key still works for now, with a line in the log asking for the token), Search shows **Picked for you** and **Because you watched …** (TMDb recommendations seeded from the Jellyfin viewing account's recent watch history; only TMDb ids leave the server, and titles already in Jellyfin or watched are left out), then Trending, Popular, and Popular on each of the region's main streaming services (`region` under `[discover]` in `usher.toml`). Each title shows where it's included with a subscription. The rows are built in the background soon after start-up and every hour, so Discover opens at once; In library and Requested marks are added fresh each time it opens, and Status → logs shows how long each build took (`discover built in …s`). Only the server talks to TMDb; posters and logos come through Usher. This product uses the TMDB API but is not endorsed or certified by TMDB; streaming data by JustWatch. Settings → **About** shows TMDb's logo with that notice, and the JustWatch credit.

**Likes and dislikes.** Each person can tap **Like** or **Dislike** in a title's details sheet, from Search or Discover; tapping the chosen one again clears it. They belong to that account alone. With at least three likes and requests between them, that account's **Picked for you** follows its own taste: up to ten seeds, its likes newest first, then the titles it asked for that are still in the library, never a disliked one. A like counts twice as much as a request, and a title whose genres are all among the dislikes' genres (and none of the likes') ranks lower. Each seed uses the same TMDb recommendations Discover already asks for, cached for a day and shared, so only TMDb ids go to TMDb. With fewer than three seeds, Picked for you is the shared one from the watch history; **Because you watched …** stays shared. Each account's row is kept in memory only, refreshed every hour and straight after a change. A disliked title leaves that account's Discover rows at once and stays for everyone else; Search still finds it, with Dislike chosen so it can be undone. **Settings → Account → Likes and dislikes** lists them, newest first and 50 at a time (**Show more** for the next), each with **Undo**. An account can keep up to 500; past that a new one is refused with a message to undo some first, and none is dropped by itself. Likes are never written to `discover.json`, Status details or the logs, and they are deleted with the account.

Search shows titles as they arrive: matches already in the library straight away, then TV and movies as Sonarr and Radarr each answer, merged into one list without repeats. Each lookup is kept for 10 minutes, so retyping a search is instant (badges are still worked out fresh), and a search that's been replaced by newer typing is skipped rather than run. How long each lookup takes is in Status → logs.

Search results carry short badges: **In library**, **Requested** (asked for, nothing downloaded yet) and **On <service>** (included with a subscription, free or with ads in the region). Movies show IMDb, Rotten Tomatoes and Metacritic scores from Radarr's own lookup, so no extra key is needed; shows, and movies IMDb hasn't scored, show TMDb's score once at least 20 people have voted. Discover posters carry a tick when a title is in the library and a clock when it has been asked for; these marks follow Get it, removals and tidy-ups straight away, on every device. Menus above the results filter by type, score (IMDb, else TMDb), release year and streaming (on a service, not streaming, or one named service), and sort by best match, highest score or newest; Discover takes the first three. Filters are kept per device. Streaming badges come from the same cached TMDb details as the detail sheet; any TMDb doesn't return within 6 seconds simply has no badge.

## Away from home

Usher is reachable only at home or through Tailscale. If it can't reach the server within a few seconds of opening (the service worker opens the cached app after 4 seconds rather than waiting on an address that leads nowhere), it shows **Can't reach home** with a **Connect Tailscale** button and keeps checking every 5 seconds and whenever it comes back to the screen, so it clears by itself once Tailscale is on. The button runs a Shortcut named `Usher Connect` (Tailscale's **Connect** action, then **Open URLs** with Usher's address), set up once per device; the screen explains how, including a short wait so Tailscale is connected before Usher reopens. When `server.shortcut_url` is set to an iCloud Shortcuts link, Settings has **Add the Connect shortcut** (iPhone and iPad), a one-tap install of the ready-made Shortcut. Tailscale's VPN On Demand (every Wi-Fi except home's, and mobile data) avoids the screen altogether.

## Posters

Sonarr and Radarr's own posters are used first. When they have none (often a show that hasn't aired), Usher asks TMDb for the title's poster (then any poster TMDb holds in English or no language, then a backdrop) by its TMDb id, or a show's TheTVDB id, and serves that through the same allow-listed fetch as search posters. Misses are remembered for 6 hours; the fallback is cached on devices for 6 hours, so Sonarr or Radarr's own picture takes over once they have one. Search results without a picture get TMDb's too.

A poster is never shown half loaded. The server checks every picture it fetches is whole (a JPEG ends with its end marker, a PNG with its last chunk, and the length matches what was promised); a cut-off one is fetched once more, then replaced by TMDb's poster or a short-lived "not found", and never kept on a device. The server log names each item whose picture came back cut off, and `/healthz` notes it once it repeats. In the app, each tile shows the title until its picture has arrived and decoded whole, then puts the finished picture in (tried once more, then the stand-in, then the title); pictures already shown are reused when a screen redraws. Every poster address comes from one place on the server (`usher/titles.py`) and carries an art version, raised when copies already on devices must not be used again.

## Opening at once

Every screen opens with something on it. The app keeps the last copy of Library, Upcoming, Discover, Tidy up and of what Home and Status show, draws it as soon as the server has confirmed the session, and refreshes it in the background; a screen visited again shows its copy while the new one arrives. Copies are written at most every 10 seconds, kept for a week, and only shown to the same account on the same version of the app.

Artwork is asked for at the width it is drawn (up to twice that in pixels), and the server has each source make that size itself: Jellyfin resizes on request, Sonarr and Radarr keep a small and a large poster, and TMDb offers a set of widths. The service worker keeps the pictures on the device, answers from there at once and refreshes a copy in the background once older than the server allows. It keeps at most 40 MB or 800 pictures and drops the least recently shown first.

While the device is idle, and never in Low Data Mode or on a very slow connection, Usher fetches what is likely next: the other tabs' lists, the rest of the rows on screen and the next screenful of artwork, and the details of a Discover title whose card is on screen (two at a time).

Signing out, a session ended on the server, or another account signing in deletes every copy and picture on the device.

## Status and Settings

**Status** answers one question: is everything working? It shows the summary card (with a row for each part that needs attention), **Health** (Downloads, Search, TV, Movies and Watching, then **Logs**), **Storage** (with **Tidy up** as its last row). The round account button with your initials at the top right of Status opens Settings; on a wide screen the "Signed in as …" button at the bottom of the sidebar does too.

**Settings** opens over Status, with a back button to it, and holds what each person changes for themselves: **Account** (name and who is signed in, and **Likes and dislikes**, to review and undo them), **Device** (notifications and their options, **Send a test notification**, and **Open titles in**), **Devices** (every signed-in device and **Sign out other devices**), **About** (version, **Changelog**, **Reload the app**, and the TMDb and JustWatch credits), **Advanced** (collapsed) and, last, **Sign out**. A live update never redraws Settings while it is open. Where Usher points at the device's own settings, it says "the Settings app".

## Notifications

Web Push to each device that turns them on (Settings → Device). On some devices this needs the app on the Home Screen.

| Kind | Default | Sent when |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Ready to watch | On | A request reaches *Ready to watch*, or new episodes of a followed show land in Jellyfin (several at once are grouped) |
| Download problems | On | A download fails or can't be imported (not when it's only waiting for space) |
| Storage getting full | On | Free space is under 30 GB, at most once a day |
| Server needs attention | First account only | Part of the server has been unhappy for 30 minutes, at most twice a day |

The app icon badge shows how many things are ready to watch. Payloads are encrypted per device (RFC 8291) and signed with the server's own key (RFC 8292, stored in `data/vapid.pem`); only the push services of the major browser makers are accepted as destinations. On first start, Usher marks the existing library as already seen so nobody gets a flood.

Nothing is sent when an update finishes. Settings → Advanced, collapsed by default, shows the last update result, and Settings → About → Changelog lists what changed in every version that changed something you would notice, newest first (read from `CHANGELOG.md`). It never appears on the attention list or anywhere else.

## Watching a title

A title that is in Jellyfin has one **Watch** button on its details sheet and a "…" menu next to it. By default **Watch** opens the title in the Jellyfin app (`swiftfin://<server id>/<user id>/item/<item id>`, which needs the server added and signed in as the shared account) and the menu offers **Open in browser**. Settings → Device has **Open titles in** (**Jellyfin app** or **Website**): it changes what **Watch** does and the menu offers the other way. The choice is kept on the device. If only one way in is known, **Watch** uses it and the menu is hidden; if neither, there is no button. `web/watch.js` picks the links and `web/menu.js` is the menu.

## Updates on devices

The app checks the server's build when it opens and whenever it comes back to the screen. The build is the version plus a short fingerprint of the app's files, so it changes after every deploy that changes the app, not only after a release. If the server runs a different build, the app reloads straight away when nothing is open, or shows **A new version of Usher is ready · Reload** otherwise. Settings → About shows the version and build, and has **Reload the app** too. The server writes the build into `web/updater.js` and `web/sw.js` when it serves them (`usher/stamp.py`), so the service worker's saved copy of the app is replaced with each new build and a phone never mixes new and old files; the running server is the only place the build comes from.
